## Why the Saw: Genesis Setting Breaks Franchise Tradition

The Saw films built their reputation on grimy, present-day industrial rooms: dripping pipes, flickering fluorescents, and a puppet on a tricycle. The Saw: Genesis setting throws nearly all of that out the window — and that is exactly why it stands out. Instead of a modern warehouse, this asymmetrical horror game takes place in 1920s London, shortly after the end of World War I.

That single decision reshapes almost everything. It changes the architecture, the costumes, the mechanics of the traps, and even the identity of the villain. There is no Jigsaw yet in the Saw: Genesis setting. In his place stands a figure called the Judge, a character with the same appetite for brutal moral lessons but none of the franchise's familiar iconography. For longtime fans, that is a genuinely bold swing — and for newcomers, it is a clean entry point into the franchise's twisted philosophy.

Below is a breakdown of what the era means for gameplay, how the 3v1 structure works, and what players should expect when a match begins.

## Inside the Saw: Genesis Setting: 1920s London After the Great War

According to hands-off demo impressions shared during Summer Game Fest 2026, the game unfolds in a version of London still recovering from the First World War. The era seeps into every design decision.

The traps feel deliberately crude. They hiss with steam, rattle violently, and look like machines assembled in a blacksmith's shop rather than a modern workshop. Survivors dress for the period — one shown in the demo wore a flapper-style dress — which makes the horror feel more like a period thriller than a slasher sequel. Even the announcement trailer leaned on that legacy: Tobin Bell narrated it, a nod to the films without placing John Kramer anywhere near the 1920s.

That tension is the point. The Judge is not a copy of Jigsaw; the Judge is the idea that eventually becomes Jigsaw.

## How the Saw: Genesis Setting Shapes Every Match

The era is not just a coat of paint — it dictates how matches flow. Because the technology is analog and the environment is claustrophobic, information becomes the most valuable resource on the map.

When a match begins, the level is procedurally generated. The Judge instantly sees the full layout, while the three survivors must explore to push back the fog of war. The Judge also gets exclusive corridors and elevators, giving them fast routes to cut off escape attempts or slip out of danger. Survivors, by contrast, must rely on teamwork, scattered weapons, and the fact that an overconfident Judge can be overwhelmed.

That asymmetry creates a very specific rhythm: quiet scouting, sudden violence, and frantic recoveries.

### Judge vs. Survivors: The Core Asymmetry

| Element | The Judge | The Survivors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Map knowledge | Full layout revealed at the start | Must clear fog of war by exploring |
| Mobility | Exclusive corridors and elevators | Standard routes, plus penalties if injured |
| Offense | Traps, toxin syringes, chutes | Weapons scattered around the level |
| Best strategy | Isolate one survivor and break the group | Stay together and punish careless plays |
| Weakness | Vulnerable when caught alone | Splitting up is usually fatal |

In one demo sequence, a survivor triggered a trap that briefly immobilized them. The Judge appeared moments later, injected a toxin that knocked the character unconscious, carried them to a chute, and dropped them below — where they woke inside a rehabilitation trap. That whole chain of events took seconds, which tells you how quickly a coordinated team can fall apart.

## Rehabilitation Traps: The Price of Survival in the Saw: Genesis Setting

Rehabilitation traps are the game's version of the death machines from the films, and they are where the Saw: Genesis setting feels most familiar. Once you are inside one, you get two choices, and neither is free.

You can wait for a teammate to rescue you, gambling that they can reach you before the timer runs out. Or you can free yourself by sacrificing a body part. That second option is permanent for the remainder of the match. Early impressions highlight two examples: losing a foot impairs your movement, while losing fingers means jobs that one person used to handle now require two.

In practice, that turns every trap into a team-wide decision. Someone has to volunteer, and someone has to live with the consequences.

| Sacrifice a foot | Frees you instantly | Movement is impaired for the rest of the match |

### Design Choices That Separate Saw: Genesis From the Pack

| Design choice | Why it matters in play |

## Saw: Genesis vs. Other Asymmetrical Horror Games

The asymmetrical horror space is crowded, and the co-developers at Bloober Team and Anshar Studios have said plainly that they do not want their game to be a copycat. Early hands-off impressions from [Shacknews' Summer Game Fest coverage of Saw: Genesis](http://www.shacknews.com/article/149592-saw-genesis-impressions-summer-game-fest) back that up: the game's identity comes from its period, not from borrowed mechanics.

The comparison table below outlines how the title approaches familiar genre tropes.

| Common asymmetrical horror convention | How the Saw: Genesis setting handles it |
| --- | --- |
| A killer already established as an icon | The Judge is a pre-Jigsaw original, so nobody knows the rules yet |
| Modern or timeless environments | Period London, complete with post-war decay and period clothing |
| Large survivor squads | Three survivors, which raises the stakes on every loss |
| Fixed, memorized maps | Procedural generation plus fog of war keeps matches unpredictable |
| Punishment that resets each round | Injuries from rehabilitation traps persist for the whole match |

That last row is the biggest differentiator. Most games in the genre let you shake off a bad moment. Here, a bad moment follows you to the end of the round.

## Tips for Surviving Your First Matches in the Saw: Genesis Setting

New players tend to die in the first three minutes. Here is how to avoid that, based on early hands-off impressions and the design details revealed so far.

- **Scout before you loot.** Survivors start blind while the Judge sees everything. Spend the opening minute mapping exits and trap-heavy rooms.
- **Move as a unit.** Weapons scattered around the level only matter if two or three survivors can gang up on the Judge at once.
- **Punish overextension.** A Judge who chases too deep into an open room can be swarmed. That window closes fast.
- **Decide trap policy early.** Agree before the match who sacrifices and who rescues, so nobody hesitates in the moment.
- **Respect the chutes.** Being knocked out and dropped below puts you straight into a rehabilitation trap with no backup.
- **Watch for steam and rattling.** Rudimentary traps telegraph themselves with noise, so slow down in tight corridors.

### Quick Reference: Role Priorities

| Mid match | Group up, collect weapons | Isolate one survivor and carry them to a chute |
| Late match | Escort injured teammates | Guard rehabilitation traps and cut rescue routes |

## FAQ: Common Questions About the Saw: Genesis Setting

**When is Saw: Genesis set?**
The game takes place in 1920s London, shortly after the end of World War I. That places it decades before the events of the films.

**Is Jigsaw in the game?**
No. The Saw: Genesis setting introduces the Judge instead, a character who delivers the same style of moral punishment. Tobin Bell narrated the announcement trailer as a tribute to the films.

**How many players does a match support?**
It is a 3v1 asymmetrical game: three survivors against one Judge, with matches typically lasting 10 to 15 minutes.

**Do injuries carry over between matches?**
Body-part sacrifices from rehabilitation traps last for the remainder of that match only. Each new round starts fresh with a newly generated map.

**Who is developing Saw: Genesis?**
Bloober Team and Anshar Studios are co-developing the game, and both studios have said they want it to feel distinct from other asymmetrical horror titles.

## The Bottom Line

The Saw: Genesis setting is not nostalgia bait. By pushing the franchise back a century, the developers free themselves from Jigsaw's shadow and build a horror sandbox where traps are mechanical, information is scarce, and every escape attempt costs something. Whether you are a longtime fan of the films or a seasoned asymmetrical horror player, the era is the reason to pay attention — and the reason matches feel different from everything else in the genre.
