### Three Features Players Keep Mixing Up

A lot of confusion comes from treating three separate features as one. They are not the same, and an announcement about one tells you almost nothing about the others:

- **Online multiplayer** — confirmed. The game is designed around playing with other people over the internet.
- **Crossplay** — unconfirmed. This would mean players on different platforms can share the same matches.
- **Cross-progression** — unconfirmed. This would mean your progress, unlocks, or purchases follow you between systems.

A developer can support one without the others. Plenty of games offer crossplay but no cross-progression, and some offer cross-progression between platforms that can never match together. Until an official statement names the specific feature, assume nothing.

**Don't assume crossplay by genre.** The fact that other asymmetrical horror titles support cross-platform play does not obligate Saw Genesis to do the same. Network architecture, platform certification, and party systems are all separate engineering problems, and they are typically solved late in development.

## How the 3-vs-1 Format Makes Platform Support a Big Deal

Saw Genesis is built around an asymmetrical power relationship rather than a standard team deathmatch format. One player controls the Judge, who oversees the trial from the shadows, while three Accused players cooperate to solve challenges, search for items, and escape before the match ends.

The Judge is not designed as an unstoppable combat character. The store description emphasizes that the Judge is physically vulnerable and must lean on preparation, hidden routes, toxins, traps, noise detection, and an Accomplice. That creates a system where information and positioning can matter as much as direct confrontation.

The Accused carry a different set of responsibilities. They share resources, coordinate rescues, and sometimes decide whether to sacrifice part of a character's body to escape a dangerous situation. Those sacrifices can leave lasting injuries that slow movement or reduce puzzle and combat effectiveness for the rest of the match.

| Role | Team size | Main objective | Key tools |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Judge | 1 | Rehabilitate the Accused through controlled trials | Traps, toxins, hidden corridors, noise detection |
| Accused | 3 | Complete challenges and escape the labyrinth | Teamwork, scavenging, keys, rescues |

### Procedural Maps and Lasting Injuries

Procedural maps mean routes and layouts can shift between matches, so fixed memorization is less reliable than clear communication and quick adaptation. For a three-player Accused team, that raises the value of voice chat and shared callouts — the kind of coordination that gets harder when friends are split across different systems.

Lasting injuries add a second layer. A player who sacrifices a limb to survive may spend the rest of the round at reduced effectiveness, which forces the team to redistribute jobs on the fly. Again, this rewards tight communication.

### Why the Role Split Changes the Crossplay Conversation

If crossplay is ever added, party coordination will be most valuable for Accused teams, where three players need to share information continuously. Judge players benefit from cross-platform play too, but their edge comes from information control, route denial, and timing rather than from constant chatter. That asymmetry is worth remembering when evaluating future announcements — a feature that seems minor for one role can be decisive for the other.

## How to Track Saw Genesis Crossplay Updates Without Guessing

Crossplay details often change during Early Access. Network architecture, party systems, and platform plans get refined while developers test matchmaking at scale — which means a "no" today can become a "yes" a year from now. The reliable approach is to watch official channels and read announcements precisely.

1. **Check the official store description.** The Steam page is updated by the publisher and is the primary source for platform and network details.
2. **Look for a developer announcement.** Patch notes, dev blogs, and roadmap posts usually carry platform news before it reaches the store page.
3. **Verify party and matchmaking rules.** "Crossplay" and "crossplay parties" are different features. Confirm which one is being described.
4. **Check account and progression details.** If multiple platforms are supported, look for separate information about account linking, saved progress, and purchased content.

| Update wording | Likely meaning | What to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-platform matchmaking | Players from multiple platforms may share matchmaking pools | Whether friend invites are included |
| Crossplay parties | Friends can likely form mixed-platform groups | Account requirements and restrictions |
| Cross-progression | Progress may transfer between systems | Linked accounts and eligible content |
| Platform-specific matchmaking | Players may be separated by system | Whether crossplay can be enabled manually |

### Questions a Real Announcement Should Answer

When a crossplay statement finally arrives, it should resolve several practical questions rather than just confirming a buzzword:

- Which platforms can play together?
- Can players invite friends across platforms?
- Is voice chat shared between platforms?
- Are matchmaking pools combined by default?
- Can players disable crossplay if they want to?
- Does progression follow the player between systems?

If an announcement leaves most of these unanswered, treat it as incomplete and wait for clarification. A vague promise is not a feature.
