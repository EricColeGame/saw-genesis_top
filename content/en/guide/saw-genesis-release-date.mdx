## Why Saw Genesis Is Launching in Early Access First

In the studio's own Early Access Q&A, the team explained that the primary reason for launching early is to stress-test a very specific power dynamic: a Judge who is clever but physically killable, paired against three Accused who are individually fragile but strong when they cooperate.

That is a balance problem you cannot solve on a spreadsheet. Real matches generate real data, and the developers want that data before they lock in the direction of future mechanics.

| Phase | What the studio says | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Early Access launch | All core mechanics plus initial content | You can learn the complete gameplay loop |
| Early Access period | Roughly one year, adjusted by feedback | Systems, balance, and content will shift |
| Full release | Expanded roster and locations | A larger, more polished version of the same game |

The studio has also been upfront that it doesn't want to drag out Early Access unnecessarily — but it also won't cut the period short if the game isn't ready. That's a reasonable position for a multiplayer title where matchmaking population and balance are intertwined.

## Judge vs. Accused: What You'll Actually Play

Set in the grim aftermath of World War I, SAW: Genesis drops you into a 3v1 confrontation on procedurally generated maps. One player takes the role of the Judge, an early architect of the philosophy that would later define Jigsaw. The other three play the Accused, fighting to escape a trap-filled labyrinth.

| Aspect | The Judge | The Accused |
|---|---|---|
| Team size | 1 | 3 |
| Physical power | Vulnerable; cannot win by force alone | Can fight back with coordination and weapons |
| Signature tools | Hidden corridors, noise-detection map, hallucinogenic gas, paralyzing toxins, summonable Accomplice | Puzzles, scavenged items, keys, sacrifice mechanics |
| Objective | Force a choice between life and limb | Escape before time runs out |
| Maps | Procedurally generated | Procedurally generated |

The twist that separates this from other asymmetrical horror games is that the Judge isn't a damage sponge. You can't simply out-muscle the Accused. Instead, you manipulate the trial from the shadows, using toxins to disorient and an Accomplice to drag prisoners into Rehabilitation Traps.

For the Accused, every injury carries a permanent cost for that match. Take damage to an arm and puzzles become harder to complete and fighting back gets riskier. Injure a leg and you move slower, making you easier to corner. Survival is possible — but you may not walk away intact.

### Why Procedural Maps Matter for Replayability

Because routes shift between matches, memorization only gets you so far. Plans break, and improvisation becomes the real skill ceiling. That design choice is a big part of why the studio wants live balancing data before committing to a final version.
